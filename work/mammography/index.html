Selected work · Imaging & biosignals

Explainable breast cancer detection without dense annotation

Make the patch the unit of evidence, and the model points at the image by construction.

Figure
The workEvery entry here carries the same four slots.
The question

Screening mammography models are usually trained on exhaustively annotated data, which is expensive and scarce, and usually explain themselves afterwards with a heatmap. Both are avoidable.

What we found

Training runs as a curriculum over patch scales, from 128 pixels through 256 and 512 to the full 1024-pixel image, with one network carried through every stage.

Because the unit of evidence is a patch, the output localises to a region of the mammogram by construction rather than through a post-hoc attribution. Annotation cost falls at the same time, since the supervision does not have to be dense.

What would sink it

If accuracy had come from the full-image stage alone, the curriculum would be decoration and the patch-level evidence would not be evidence.
