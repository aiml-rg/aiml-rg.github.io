Self-reports do not identify self-models

A model describing its own reasoning does not, on its own, tell you what that reasoning was.

Figure
The workEvery entry here carries the same four slots.
The question

When a model explains why it answered as it did, does the explanation constrain what the underlying computation could have been? A great deal of interest in chain-of-thought and self-explanation quietly assumes that it does.

What we found

Not on its own. A self-report is evidence about behaviour in a prompt environment, and a mechanism that never reads the intervened hidden state can reproduce exactly the same reports as one that does. The two are observationally equivalent and separate only under intervention.

The test that separates them is cheap. Hold the named intervention fixed and shift the demonstration environment around it. Across three open instruction models, wrong-source demonstrations pull the reports toward the source answer family, and binding the mechanism explicitly reduces that pull. So the accuracy of a self-report benchmark is not yet evidence of an autonomous report mechanism, and the fix is an invariance test rather than an abandoned method.

What would sink it

If wrong-source demonstrations had left the reports unmoved, the reports would already be bound to the intervention and the invariance test would be unnecessary.

Paper

P. M. Konrad, T. Tanyel, S. Ayvaz · ICML 2026 Workshop on Philosophy of Machine Learning · oral
